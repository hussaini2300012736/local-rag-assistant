The 2026 Formula 1 season introduced the biggest technical regulation overhaul in the sport's history, affecting the chassis, power units, and aerodynamics all at once. Cars became smaller and lighter: wheelbase was shortened by 200mm, overall width was reduced by 100mm, and minimum weight dropped by 30kg compared to the previous generation of cars. The wide, ground-effect floor tunnels that generated most of the car's downforce in previous seasons were replaced with flatter floors, cutting downforce levels significantly and making cars harder to drive on the limit but easier to follow closely in corners.

The most significant single change was the introduction of full-time Active Aerodynamics. Instead of the old Drag Reduction System (DRS), which only opened a rear wing flap in specific zones to help overtaking, 2026 cars can adjust both their front and rear wing angles anywhere on track. On straights, a low-drag "Straight Mode" flattens the wings for higher top speed; in corners, "Corner Mode" closes the flaps back down for grip. Every driver can use this on every lap, not just when chasing another car.
