Pirelli's tyres were redesigned for 2026 to match the smaller, lighter cars. Both front and rear tyres shrank: fronts became 25mm narrower and about 15mm smaller in overall diameter, while rears became 30mm narrower and roughly 10mm smaller in diameter. The wheel rim itself stayed at 18 inches, but for the first time teams can choose their own rim manufacturer instead of using a single spec supplier, giving them a new area to fine-tune tyre temperature management.

The dry-weather compound range also shrank, dropping from six compounds to five. The softest compound, C6, was removed because testing showed too small a performance gap between it and C5 to justify keeping it. The 2026 range now runs from C1 (hardest) to C5 (softest), with Pirelli deliberately widening the performance gap between neighbouring compounds to create clearer strategic differences between them. Wet and intermediate tyres were also revised to handle water better and warm up faster, reducing the need for tyre warmers.

Because the new active-aero cars generate much more variable loads through corners and straights than previous ground-effect cars, the tyres had to be built to handle rapidly changing forces without overheating or losing stability. Teams tested these tyres throughout 2025 using mule cars modified to mimic 2026 aerodynamic behavior, since real 2026 cars weren't ready in time for early tyre development.
