Power units changed dramatically for 2026. The core layout stayed a 1.6-litre turbocharged V6, but the balance between combustion and electrical power shifted close to 50-50, up from roughly 80-20 in previous seasons. This tripled the electrical motor's contribution while reducing the combustion engine's output, and the cars now run on advanced sustainable fuel instead of standard racing fuel.

Because so much more power now comes from the battery and electric motor, energy management became a central part of race strategy. Drivers can choose between different energy deployment tools during a race: a Boost Button for manual attacking or defending bursts of power, and an Overtake Mode that activates when a car is within one second of the car ahead at a specific point on track, granting extra harvested and deployable electrical energy for a full lap. Getting energy management wrong during a race can cost a driver significantly more than in previous seasons, since mistakes compound as the battery depletes.
