With the grid expanding to 11 teams and 22 cars in 2026, the format of qualifying changed slightly to accommodate the extra cars. Q1 now runs 18 minutes and eliminates the six slowest cars (rather than five), who fill grid positions 17 through 22. Q2 runs 15 minutes and again eliminates six cars, filling positions 11 through 16. The remaining 10 cars go through to Q3, which was extended slightly to 13 minutes, to fight for pole position. The same six-car elimination change was also applied to Sprint Qualifying.

The points system for race finishes and sprint results stayed the same as previous seasons: points go to the top ten finishers in a Grand Prix, and a smaller point allocation goes to the top eight finishers in a Sprint. One scoring change did happen, though: the bonus point for setting the fastest lap of a race was officially removed. It had been criticized for letting teams outside the points make a free late-race pit stop purely to steal a point without any real risk, which felt disconnected from genuine race pace.

The 2026 calendar kept 24 Grands Prix and six Sprint weekends, including new Sprint venues. Safety also saw a small but notable addition: cars now have lights fitted to the outer edges of their rear-view mirrors, which activate in wet conditions so other drivers can see a car more clearly through spray or if it spins, specifically to help prevent dangerous side-impact collisions.
