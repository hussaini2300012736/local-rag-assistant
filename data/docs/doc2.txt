An F1 car's power unit combines a turbocharged 1.6-litre V6 engine with hybrid energy recovery systems. These systems capture energy from braking and from the turbocharger, storing it in a battery and deploying it back to the wheels for extra power. This hybrid setup makes the power unit far more fuel-efficient than earlier pure combustion engines while still producing enormous performance.

Tyres are another major factor in strategy. Teams choose between different compounds of varying hardness: softer tyres grip better and are faster over a single lap but wear out quickly, while harder tyres last longer but offer less grip. Races typically require at least one pit stop to change tyres, and choosing the right strategy around tyre wear can decide the outcome of a race.
